> DRAFT · Phase XLIV · 2026-11-26. All exhibit prices and workflows are fictional, explicitly assumed. Not economic statistics, normative keyboard layout, actual programs or validated hardware.

## Relative purchase cost
Use L = one month of hypothetical local skilled-worker gross earnings in the year of purchase. This is a scenario ratio, not a historical currency, inflation series, income estimate or conversion. Machine bundles differ: institutional configurations include basic terminal/storage and omit building/ongoing staff; personal configurations include basic display and input where appropriate; M05 excludes TV and games; M10 excludes recurring service; M11 includes tower and monitor. Prices are selected to express plausible segmentation and are not derived from production costs.

## A proposed educational bilingual keyboard
This exhibit offers an alphabetic rather than a frequency-optimized layout. Physical positions are numbered independently of encoding; the following 3x8 alphabet block is only a teaching fixture, not a claim of universal production keyboards. Greek and Latin mode keys are distinct from case Shift; digits, punctuation, controls, accents and modifier behavior require independent assignments.

| Mode | Row 1 | Row 2 | Row 3 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| LATINA | A B C D E F G H | I K L M N O P Q | R S T V X Y Z — |
| GRAECA | Α Β Γ Δ Ε Ζ Η Θ | Ι Κ Λ Μ Ν Ξ Ο Π | Ρ Σ Τ Υ Φ Χ Ψ Ω |

The dash indicates an unassigned letter position in LATINA, not a typed hyphen. A shared physical key may produce different identities. For CVL-54 a terminal emits a page switch when necessary; for CVL-68 it emits direct distinct character codes; for COS it selects a registered identity and serializes under a declared interchange profile. A font may draw Latin A and Greek Α alike, without making them identical. No implicit transliteration. Later scholarly accent composition, cursor navigation and final sigma need their own text policy. Real products may use optimized or local layouts. No firmware or keyboard scan code standard defined.

## A single greeting across three generations
M01: SALVE (five six-bit letter units) followed by a space and a Greek-page shift and ΛΟΓΟΣ uses twelve six-bit units before reset or end-of-record, fits two 36-bit words as a packed example; terminal reset/end handling is separate. M02: eleven direct CVL-68 bytes 52 41 4B 54 45 20 8A 8E 82 8E 91. M09: the same registered identity sequence may occupy 33 C24 octets or 11 W1 octets under the candidate legacy mapping; encoding must be declared. M11: illustrative MENTIS vocabulary may segment into four tokens, not redefine the text. ## Preservation
Retain original bytes, machine/word packing, profile revision, provenance, software and asset rights, peripherals, and any measured evidence separately. For early word-addressed machines retain packing metadata; 36-bit word streams are not inherently byte-addressed files. Distinguish original hardware, reconstructed configuration, archival modern emulator and paper design. Only XXXVI and N1 have earlier experimental executable subsets; no new exhibit was run. A common text format or compatible instruction subset cannot certify full applications. Preserve data exchange, source compatibility, binary compatibility, layout, device support and numerical equivalence as separate categories.
