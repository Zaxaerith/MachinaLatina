> Phase XLIII · 2026-11-26 · DRAFT / NOT CANON. Six invented firms, proposed founding years and business histories. Names are fictional working names, not claims about real companies or registered trademarks. No specific geopolitical borders, currency, corporate law, founders, financial statements or market shares are assumed. The 1965 founding of OFFICINA NOVA is a prologue to the 1970–2026 study.

## IND-01 — OFFICINA NOVA
Founding scenario: commercial equipment engineers and terminal suppliers cooperate to reduce the cost of replacing page-shift systems. 1968 N1 participates in the eight-bit transition; not the exclusive inventor of CVL-68. 1970s revenue comes from systems, maintenance and terminals. In the 1980s, external microcomputer makers demand independently priced processors and documentation. Candidate 1991 licensing arrangement opens a defined Maior implementation subset to other suppliers under published compatibility terms; actual patent terms, licensing rates and test procedures remain undecided. This is not an open-source ISA guarantee. Magna and Mobilis later expand the business; 2026 preferred scenario includes rival licensed implementations and alternative architectures. Risk: channel partners become competitors, and opaque firmware weakens trust. Related [Nova family history](../de-nova-1981-2026.md).

## IND-02 — ACRIBIA
Name inspired by Greek ἀκρίβεια (precision), a provisional Latinized engineering brand. University instrument engineers and specialist manufacturers form a candidate spin-out in 1976, distinct from Consortium Scientiarum. S0 prototype and S1/F48 line focus on numerical precision, support and archive fidelity. S2/PARTES workstations enter the professional market around 1989. Wider Nova adoption threatens the hardware business during the 1990s. In the preferred branch, ACRIBIA stops developing new general-purpose 48-bit workstation CPUs around 2001, maintains old systems, and ports supported PARTES services and F48 decoding libraries to Nova. No claim of binary compatibility, completed Nova IPC port or actual merger. By 2026 revenue could come from long-term scientific maintenance, instrument integration and numerical validation. This is the institutional seed for the conditional 2033 Sextaria engine study, not proof of corporate survival to 2033. Related [Sextaria history](../de-sextaria-1976-2005.md).

## IND-03 — DOMESTICA
Education-terminal supplier, initially buys processors and display components rather than manufacturing chips. 1981 candidate Minor D1 is a low-cost classroom configuration. Media-based D2 expands graphics and disks around 1986; a 1989 dispute over TABVLA compatibility forces explicit document/source/binary claims. The company backs the proposed 1991 Maior licensing initiative because a second source reduces supply dependence. 1990s PCs use shared hardware profiles and independently maintained ORDO-N distributions. In 2008–2015, Mobilis and CVSTOS support a mobile product line; a candidate owner-controlled edition competes with locked products. 2026 emphasis is repairable devices, accessible replacement parts and local software, without assuming it controls ORDO-N or CVSTOS upstream. Risk: price competition and thin hardware margins.

## IND-04 — PAGINA
A collaboration of printing engineers, type designers and academic publishers forms a firm in 1984. It commercializes a TABVLA-compatible suite; the underlying desktop concept predates and is not owned by this firm. Greek composition, glyph metrics and print fidelity support premium licenses and institutional maintenance. It does not treat CVL bytes as individual glyph cells. In the 1990s it participates in the semantic-versus-fixed-page debate and supports COS migration while offering a proprietary publishing suite. Around 2005, NODVS exchange becomes a competitive demand, not necessarily an exclusive file format. Candidate 2026 business: cross-platform document authoring, fonts and archival services, with export-oriented customers and open-source competitors. Risk: proprietary extensions impede document portability.

## IND-05 — ARCADIA
Entertainment-board supplier turns toward cartridge-style LVDVS-I products in 1983–1986. It licenses game publishing tools and sells consoles; a lower hardware margin can be recovered only if sufficient game sales occur. Later profiles cover scrolling, handhelds, fixed-function 3D and digital distribution. Separate game licensing, platform access and hardware compatibility: buying a game on LVDVS II does not prove LVDVS III execution. Around 1997 candidate partnership with PRISMA supplies 3D components under a console-specific contract, not automatically compatible with a PC board. Around 2012 an independent developer program reduces entry costs; closed store and preservation policies remain contentious. In 2026 both specialist gaming hardware and PC/mobile competition persist. LVDVS remains a technical family, not a single mandatory global brand.

## IND-06 — PRISMA
Founded in 1992 from engineering visualization board design, serving scientific workstations and later personal PCs. Offers candidate FIGVRA-compatible accelerator boards around 1995–1998; host-side geometry and native drivers still required. Around 1997 supplies a console-specific device to ARCADIA and continues a separate PC line, balancing exclusivity with volume. 2005–2015 research shifts to programmable pipelines; 2015–2026 adds digital matrix engines, sometimes separate from graphics. It competes in hardware and developer tooling rather than owning FIGVRA or MATRICES. Actual shader ISA, driver ABI, fabrication partners and throughput not specified. Risk: high development costs and software ecosystem dependence.

## Institutions are not corporations
CCL and CSM are proposed text-standard bodies; Consortium Scientiarum is a research constituency; Collegium Machinarum and Officinae Telegraphicae are earlier interests/constituencies. LIBERTAS CODICVM is a movement, FORVM CODICVM is a class of collaborative platforms. None is assigned to these six firms. ORDO-N, PARTES, CVSTOS, TABVLA, FIGVRA and the processor family names are not asserted as corporate-exclusive trademarks. Six firms are a narrative sample, not the entire global industry; independent manufacturers and communities, especially those serving other writing systems, remain possible.
