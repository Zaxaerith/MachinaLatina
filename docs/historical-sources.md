# Historical Sources / 现实史实依据

This bibliography identifies **real** references that constrain or inspire the alternate history. Real-world dates, devices and standards must not be presented as fictional CVL product history.

## Computing and characters

1. **IBM Archives — IBM 704**. IBM documentation and corporate history on early scientific computing, including its 36-bit design and role in FORTRAN. https://www.ibm.com/history/704
2. **IBM Archives — System/360**. The System/360 family provides a real historical reference point for byte-oriented computer families in 1964. https://www.ibm.com/history/system-360
3. **The Computer History Museum — FORTRAN**. Historical information on the first FORTRAN implementation and its constraints. https://www.computerhistory.org/
4. **RFC 3629 — UTF-8, a transformation format of ISO 10646**, November 2003. Useful for studying byte compatibility and encoded text—not a source for our fictional CVL mappings. https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc3629
5. **The Unicode Standard — Greek and Coptic / Greek Extended**. Relevant to Greek final sigma, combining accents, and the distinction between code points and glyphs. https://www.unicode.org/charts/

## Greco-Roman culture

6. **Quintilian, Institutio Oratoria 1.1** — Roman discussion of early Greek and Latin education. Use a critical translation and context when citing this; it does not imply all Romans shared a single view.
7. **Greco-Roman bilingual inscriptions and educational scholarship** — examples showing Greek and Latin alongside each other, with attention to period, region and social class.

## Standards-development comparisons

8. **ITU-T International Telegraph Alphabet No. 2 (ITA2)** — letter/figure shift mechanisms offer a *real* precedent for fictional early page switching. Do not conflate ITA2's five-bit codes with CVL-54's invented six-bit codes.

## Research integrity rules

- Supply a specific primary document, official reference or reputable scholarly source when moving a historical point into a narrative.
- Record discrepancies and dates rather than inventing precise historical quotations.
- CVL-54, CVL-63, CVL-68, TRANFOR and Machina XXXVI are fictional project constructs.
- Latin technical coinages in this repository should be checked by qualified classicists before being described as idiomatic or historically attested.
